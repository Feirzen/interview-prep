COACH: Savannah is Georgia's first city. In February of seventeen thirty-three, James Oglethorpe landed on a bluff above the Savannah River with about a hundred and twenty colonists. He made peace with Tomochichi, the leader of the Yamacraw people, and that friendship is a big part of the city's founding story. Savannah was the colony's first capital.
COACH: Oglethorpe laid the city out on a grid of wards, each one built around a public square. That plan is still there. Today there are twenty-two squares downtown, shaded by live oaks and Spanish moss, and it's one of the most famous city plans in the country. That's what you'll see from the trolley tomorrow.
COACH: Savannah grew rich as a port, mostly on cotton. The cotton gin was invented on a plantation just outside the city in seventeen ninety-three. And that wealth came out of enslaved labor, which the city is more and more honest about today. You'll likely hear some of that on the tour.
COACH: In December of eighteen sixty-four, at the end of his march to the sea, General Sherman took Savannah. Instead of burning it, he sent President Lincoln a telegram offering him the city as a Christmas gift. That's a big reason so much of the old city survived.
COACH: The next chapter is preservation. In the nineteen fifties, the city was tearing down historic buildings. When the Davenport House was about to be demolished, a group of local women saved it and started the Historic Savannah Foundation. That kicked off one of the most successful preservation movements in America.
COACH: A few more you might hear about. Juliette Gordon Low founded the Girl Scouts here in nineteen twelve. The Savannah College of Art and Design, S C A D, moved into dozens of old buildings starting in the late seventies and helped bring downtown back to life. Forsyth Park, with its big white fountain, is the city's front lawn. River Street runs along the water, with old cotton warehouses turned into shops and restaurants. And today the Port of Savannah is one of the busiest container ports in the country.
COACH: Now the practical side. South's campus is on Mall Boulevard, on the south side of town near Oglethorpe Mall. Not downtown. Historic downtown is about fifteen or twenty minutes north, and Tybee Island, Savannah's beach, is about half an hour east. Winters are mild, which you and Ri will love. Summers are hot and humid, and hurricane season runs June through November, so that's worth asking students about.
COACH: For church, there's a Savannah stake, and there's a ward building on Montgomery Cross Road, ten minutes or so from campus. Charleston is under two hours away, Jacksonville is about two, and Atlanta is about four. Lots of weekend adventures close by.
