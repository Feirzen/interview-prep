How the 28 months are built

Semesters 1 to 4 · foundation

About 16 months
  • Anesthesia principles, pharm, physiology
  • Heavy simulation. 800+ hours of classroom and sim total
  • 3-semester equipment and monitoring series (machine, vaporizers, vents, checkout)

Semesters 5 to 7 · clinical year

Final 12 months
  • Year-long rotations, including outside sites
  • Review courses run alongside
  • Senior seminar on teaching skills
Memory hook: 28 months, 7 semesters, 116 credits. 800 hours in class and sim, 2,200+ with patients.

What makes UNM different

  • First AA program in New Mexico. State approved it in 2023, first class started August 2024
  • That first class is finishing its clinical year right about now, so there are no board pass rates yet
  • Home base is UNM Hospital, the state's academic medical center and only Level I trauma center
  • Small class means lots of faculty time and sim time per student

Why it fits you

  • Close to Utah, same Mountain time, same desert West
  • Big Spanish-speaking population. About half of New Mexico is Hispanic, so your Spanish gets used every day
  • Underserved and rural patients all over the state. That's the population you've said you want
  • Being in an early class means you help shape the program

Don't ask (it's on the site)

  • How long is it? How many credits?
  • What's tuition?
  • What's the pass rate? (there isn't one yet)
  • What does it take to apply?

Do ask

  • What did you learn from the first class that you've changed for later ones?
  • Where do the outside clinical-year rotations happen, and how are they assigned?
  • Where are the first grads getting hired, and are New Mexico jobs opening up for CAAs?
  • Students: what's it like being one of the first classes?

Tuition is UNM's posted total for the 116-credit plan and may change. Class size is from 2024 and could be bigger now. Everything else is from UNM's MSA program pages and its summer 2025 newsletter.
